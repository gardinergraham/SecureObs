# SecureObs Product Overview Video

## Production storyboard, narration and recording checklist

**Target duration:** 6 minutes 15 seconds  
**Audience:** Care-home owners, registered managers, mental-health services, secure services, hospitals and NHS organisations  
**Primary goal:** Encourage the viewer to book a tailored demonstration  
**Format:** 16:9 landscape, 1920 × 1080, 25 or 30 frames per second  
**Data:** SecureObs Demonstration Organisation and fictional records only

---

## The demonstration story

The video follows one connected workflow:

> Sarah, a fictional nurse, signs into Demonstration Ward. The ward overview shows that Patient B has a NEWS2 assessment and a patient task requiring attention. Sarah records the assessment, adds a progress note and completes the action. The information becomes available to the wider clinical record, shift handover and manager governance view. A manager then reviews the patient from a desktop, updates the care plan and checks the organisation's CQC evidence readiness.

This story demonstrates how SecureObs connects frontline care, clinical documentation, operational oversight and governance.

## Scene 5 — NEWS2 and clinical escalation

**Time:** 1:55–2:30

# Voiceover guidance

- Record the narration after editing a rough version of the screen footage.
- Use a quiet room and the same microphone for the whole recording.
- Speak naturally at approximately 125–140 words per minute.
- Leave short pauses between sentences for editing.
- Avoid exaggerated sales language.
- Say “supports” rather than “guarantees”.
- Say “CQC evidence readiness” rather than “CQC rating”.
- Use “patient” or “person receiving care” according to the audience.
- Add accurate subtitles and manually proofread NEWS2, TESO, eMAR and CQC terminology.

---

# Editing style

- Use SecureObs navy, teal and white.
- Use simple crossfades and controlled zooms.
- Avoid rapid transitions, spinning graphics and excessive animation.
- Keep interface text sharp and readable.
- Use a tablet frame only when it helps the viewer understand the context.
- Highlight one important area at a time.
- Keep background music quiet enough that every word remains clear.
- Normalise voiceover volume across all scenes.
- Add “Demonstration data” to all clinical footage.
- Add chapter titles only where they improve orientation.
